# Round 9 - Behavioral and Project

In this round you talk about your past work, how you work with people and why you want the job. There is no code. It is commonly a 30 to 45 minute video call with a hiring manager, a senior engineer or both. Large companies may run several behavioral interviews in one loop. For example, Amazon's prep page says each interviewer in its final loop is mapped to its leadership principles.

## What the round tests
- **Ownership:** what you personally decided and built, not only what the team did.
- **Depth:** whether you really know your last project, from architecture to the hardest bug.
- **Working with people:** conflict, feedback, designers, backend teams, mentoring.
- **Learning:** failures, incidents, and what you changed afterwards.
- **Motivation:** why this company, and the quality of the questions you ask.

## How to practise with the files
1. Build your story bank first (next section). Do not open the answers yet.
2. Open the [questions file](./questions/01-behavioral-and-project.md). Take three questions a day. Set a timer (2 minutes for behavioral, 3 minutes for project questions), answer out loud and record yourself.
3. Open the [answers file](./answers/01-behavioral-and-project.md). Compare your structure with the STAR template at the top and with the outline. The outlines are examples with placeholders, not scripts. Never use a story that is not yours.
4. Write your honest answers to the eight HR questions once, and re-read them before every recruiter call.
5. Run a mock round with a friend: they pick random IDs and add two follow-up questions ("What was your part?", "What would you change?").

## Scoring rubric
Score each practice answer from 0 (missing) to 2 (strong) on five lines. Maximum 10.

| Line | A strong answer (2 points) |
| --- | --- |
| Structure | Clear Situation, Task, Action, Result in under 2 minutes |
| Ownership | "I" is clear: your decisions and your actions |
| Specifics | Real details, and a number or a clear before and after |
| Reflection | A lesson, and a habit you changed |
| Honesty and tone | No blame, no invented facts, calm voice |

8 to 10: ready. 5 to 7: rework the weak line. Below 5: rebuild the story.

## Common mistakes
- Saying only "we", so the interviewer cannot see your part.
- Skipping the result, or inventing numbers that fall apart under follow-up questions.
- Blaming a teammate, a designer or a past employer.
- Not remembering the technology, versions or numbers of your own project.
- Having no questions for the interviewer, or asking about pay and holidays first.
